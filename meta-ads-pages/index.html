Why the current page loses leads

Three things stand between an ad click and a phone call.

The current quote page was built like a desktop brochure, and phone visitors pay for it.

01

A label that looks like a button

"Keeping Chicago Homes Warm Since 1952" sits in a pill shape at the top. Visitors tap it, nothing happens, and that first wasted tap is where many of them leave.

02

A ten-field form

The form asks for country, state, city and street address before anyone has spoken to the customer. Every extra field on a phone keyboard costs leads.

03

Eighteen sections to scroll

The call button and form are pushed below a long hero, and most phone visitors never scroll far enough to find them.

What every option does

Four rules all eight designs follow.

Only buttons look like buttons

Red and pill-shaped means tappable. Labels, ratings and areas are plain text.

Action in the first screen

Your number, your Google and Yelp ratings and the form all appear before any scrolling.

Three fields at most

First name, mobile and ZIP. We collect the address on the call.

Call never more than a tap away

A bar with "Call now" stays at the bottom of the screen the whole time.

Short to act, long enough to read

Every option is four to five phone screens long. The first screen does the selling, so a visitor who is ready can call or leave a number straight away. Below it, the same five sections answer what a careful buyer still wants to know: how it works, what you fix, why choose you, the quick answers on price and timing, and where you work. Each page then ends with a final call to action.

After someone fills in the form

Every option ends on the same thank-you screen.

It confirms the request straight away, so nobody wonders whether the form worked. It also tells the customer which number you'll call from, gives a "Can't wait? Call now" button, and suggests two safe checks while they wait.

  • It lists two simple things to check: the thermostat and the furnace filter.
  • It warns anyone who smells gas to leave the house and call 911.
  • It is where Facebook records the lead, so Meta learns which ads bring in real customers.
Our recommendation

Test two at a time, and judge them on booked jobs.

The ad stays the same and only the page changes, so the result tells you which page works better. We measure the cost of each booked job, not just the cost of each form.

What we need from you

Five quick answers before launch.

1

Your winter offers

Please confirm that it's $50 off service calls, $500 off new equipment and $1,000 off a complete system, and that all three end on November 30.

2

Your founding year

Your site says 1952 and the BBB lists 1989. Once you confirm the year, we can add your heritage back as a line of text, never as a button.

3

Who answers the phone

Industry call data shows that a person picks up only about half of home-services calls. Let us know your answering hours, and whether calls outside them go to an answering service.

4

Your form system

Your form system currently requires address, state and country. We need those fields made optional so the three-field form can send leads through.

5

Your licence number

"Licensed & insured" is stronger with a licence number beside it. Please send it if you're happy to show it.
